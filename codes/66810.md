## Rationale by Dimension

### Clinical Uniqueness (-2.0)

PRK is a specialized refractive surgery but is offered by many ophthalmology practices, not unique to Mount Sinai. The facts describe its indications and safety profile, indicating it is a commodity service that Cigna could steer to other in‑network providers.

## Supporting Facts

- **[Regulatory & Pricing Benchmarks]** Photorefractive keratectomy (PRK) received FDA approval in 1995.  
  _source: <https://www.nyee.edu/care/eye/cataract-laser-vision-correction/refractive-surgery/prk>_
- **[Clinical Uniqueness]** PRK is used when LASIK is not appropriate due to chronic dry eyes or when a corneal flap is not recommended.  
  _source: <https://www.nyee.edu/care/eye/cataract-laser-vision-correction/refractive-surgery/prk>_
- **[Clinical Uniqueness]** PRK requires 1 to 2 weeks for immediate recovery.  
  _source: <https://www.nyee.edu/care/eye/cataract-laser-vision-correction/refractive-surgery/prk>_
- **[Clinical Uniqueness]** PRK results in minimal to no haze, glare, or halos.  
  _source: <https://www.nyee.edu/care/eye/cataract-laser-vision-correction/refractive-surgery/prk>_
- **[Clinical Uniqueness]** PRK is a safe and effective treatment for patients who do not qualify for LASIK.  
  _source: <https://www.nyee.edu/care/eye/cataract-laser-vision-correction/refractive-surgery/prk>_
- **[Clinical Uniqueness]** PRK is also called a flapless LASIK.  
  _source: <https://www.nyee.edu/care/eye/cataract-laser-vision-correction/refractive-surgery/prk>_
- **[Clinical Uniqueness]** PRK is safe for patients with chronic dry eyes.  
  _source: <https://www.nyee.edu/care/eye/cataract-laser-vision-correction/refractive-surgery/prk>_
- **[Clinical Uniqueness]** PRK is safe for patients with thin corneas.  
  _source: <https://www.nyee.edu/care/eye/cataract-laser-vision-correction/refractive-surgery/prk>_
- **[Clinical Uniqueness]** PRK can treat astigmatism up to 6.00 diopters.  
  _source: <https://www.nyee.edu/care/eye/cataract-laser-vision-correction/refractive-surgery/prk>_
- **[Clinical Uniqueness]** PRK can treat nearsightedness up to -12.00 or farsightedness up to +6.00.  
  _source: <https://www.nyee.edu/care/eye/cataract-laser-vision-correction/refractive-surgery/prk>_
- **[Clinical Uniqueness]** PRK is suitable for patients who are at least 21 years of age.  
  _source: <https://www.nyee.edu/care/eye/cataract-laser-vision-correction/refractive-surgery/prk>_
- **[Clinical Uniqueness]** PRK requires a stable eye prescription, with no change greater than 0.50 to 1.0 diopter over the past two years.  
  _source: <https://www.nyee.edu/care/eye/cataract-laser-vision-correction/refractive-surgery/prk>_
- **[Clinical Uniqueness]** PRK should not be performed on patients with an unstable (changing) refractive error.  
  _source: <https://www.nyee.edu/care/eye/cataract-laser-vision-correction/refractive-surgery/prk>_
- **[Clinical Uniqueness]** PRK should not be performed on patients with a history of autoimmune disease such as lupus or HIV.  
  _source: <https://www.nyee.edu/care/eye/cataract-laser-vision-correction/refractive-surgery/prk>_
- **[Clinical Uniqueness]** PRK should not be performed on patients with a history of corneal dystrophy.  
  _source: <https://www.nyee.edu/care/eye/cataract-laser-vision-correction/refractive-surgery/prk>_
- **[Clinical Uniqueness]** PRK should not be performed on patients with a history of viral infection by herpes simplex or herpes zoster.  
  _source: <https://www.nyee.edu/care/eye/cataract-laser-vision-correction/refractive-surgery/prk>_
- **[Clinical Uniqueness]** PRK should not be performed on patients with advanced glaucoma.  
  _source: <https://www.nyee.edu/care/eye/cataract-laser-vision-correction/refractive-surgery/prk>_
- **[Clinical Uniqueness]** PRK should not be performed on patients with uncontrolled diabetes.  
  _source: <https://www.nyee.edu/care/eye/cataract-laser-vision-correction/refractive-surgery/prk>_
- **[Clinical Uniqueness]** PRK removes the epithelium, about the thickness of a human hair, which regrows within five days.  
  _source: <https://www.nyee.edu/care/eye/cataract-laser-vision-correction/refractive-surgery/prk>_
- **[Clinical Uniqueness]** PRK uses an excimer laser to reshape the cornea.  
  _source: <https://www.nyee.edu/care/eye/cataract-laser-vision-correction/refractive-surgery/prk>_
- **[Clinical Uniqueness]** Wavefront-guided PRK uses wavefront eye-mapping technology that is 25 times more precise than standard prescriptions.  
  _source: <https://www.nyee.edu/care/eye/cataract-laser-vision-correction/refractive-surgery/prk>_
- **[Clinical Uniqueness]** Wavefront-guided PRK can bring patients closer to 20/20 vision or better.  
  _source: <https://www.nyee.edu/care/eye/cataract-laser-vision-correction/refractive-surgery/prk>_
- **[Clinical Uniqueness]** PRK results are irreversible, and a second procedure may be needed if over- or under-correction occurs.  
  _source: <https://www.nyee.edu/care/eye/cataract-laser-vision-correction/refractive-surgery/prk>_
- **[Clinical Uniqueness]** PRK is a better alternative if your job or lifestyle cannot accommodate a corneal flap.  
  _source: <https://www.nyee.edu/care/eye/cataract-laser-vision-correction/refractive-surgery/prk>_
