## Rationale by Dimension

### Regulatory & Pricing Benchmarks (-5.0)

CMS’s lesser‑of methodology and inclusion of non‑covered self‑administered versions inflate Part B payments for Cimzia by 13‑19 % and $400‑$600 per dose, creating a clear benchmark for Cigna to negotiate lower rates and correct the overpayment.

## Supporting Facts

- **[Regulatory & Pricing Benchmarks]** CMS uses the lesser of methodology to determine payment for Cimzia when identified as a self‑administered product in the July 2020 OIG report.  
  _source: <https://www.cms.gov/medicare/payment/part-b-drugs/asp-reporting>_
- **[Regulatory & Pricing Benchmarks]** CMS included noncovered, self-administered versions of Cimzia in Part B payment calculations, contributing to an extra $366 million paid by Medicare from 2014 through 2016.  
  _source: <https://oig.hhs.gov/reports/all/2020/loophole-in-drug-payment-rule-continues-to-cost-medicare-and-beneficiaries-hundreds-of-millions-of-dollars/>_
- **[Financial Condition]** Closing the payment loophole for Cimzia would have saved Medicare and beneficiaries nearly half a billion dollars in 2017 and 2018.  
  _source: <https://oig.hhs.gov/reports/all/2020/loophole-in-drug-payment-rule-continues-to-cost-medicare-and-beneficiaries-hundreds-of-millions-of-dollars/>_
- **[Regulatory & Pricing Benchmarks]** The payment loophole may give physicians substantial incentives to administer Cimzia instead of other drugs for the same conditions.  
  _source: <https://oig.hhs.gov/reports/all/2020/loophole-in-drug-payment-rule-continues-to-cost-medicare-and-beneficiaries-hundreds-of-millions-of-dollars/>_
- **[Regulatory & Pricing Benchmarks]** CMS includes the prices for three versions of Cimzia when calculating Part B payment amounts, but only one is intended for physician administration.  
  _source: <https://oig.hhs.gov/documents/evaluation/3210/OEI-BL-20-00100-Complete%20Report.pdf>_
- **[Regulatory & Pricing Benchmarks]** If CMS had based payment solely on the physician‑administered version of Cimzia, Medicare quarterly payment amounts would have been 13% to 19% lower in 2017 and 2018.  
  _source: <https://oig.hhs.gov/documents/evaluation/3210/OEI-BL-20-00100-Complete%20Report.pdf>_
- **[Financial Condition]** Medicare and beneficiaries would have saved $104 million over two years if payment amounts for Cimzia had been set using only physician‑administered versions.  
  _source: <https://oig.hhs.gov/documents/evaluation/3210/OEI-BL-20-00100-Complete%20Report.pdf>_
- **[Financial Condition]** The payment loophole caused Cimzia to cost approximately $400 to $600 more per dose compared to physician‑administered versions.  
  _source: <https://oig.hhs.gov/documents/evaluation/3210/OEI-BL-20-00100-Complete%20Report.pdf>_
- **[Financial Condition]** A typical Medicare beneficiary could face more than $1,000 in additional out‑of‑pocket spending per year due to the Cimzia payment loophole.  
  _source: <https://oig.hhs.gov/documents/evaluation/3210/OEI-BL-20-00100-Complete%20Report.pdf>_
- **[unclassified]** Nearly 16,000 Medicare beneficiaries had at least one claim for Cimzia paid under Part B in 2018.  
  _source: <https://oig.hhs.gov/documents/evaluation/3210/OEI-BL-20-00100-Complete%20Report.pdf>_
- **[Regulatory & Pricing Benchmarks]** As of December 2019, CMS continued to include noncovered self‑administered versions of Cimzia in Part B payment calculations.  
  _source: <https://oig.hhs.gov/documents/evaluation/3210/OEI-BL-20-00100-Complete%20Report.pdf>_
