## Rationale by Dimension

### Regulatory & Pricing Benchmarks (-4.0)

CMS’s lesser‑of methodology and the identified payment loophole for self‑administered Cimzia provide Cigna with a clear benchmark and a strong argument for reducing payments, as evidenced by the $366 million overpayment and potential savings of nearly $500 million if the loophole were closed.

## Supporting Facts

- **[Regulatory & Pricing Benchmarks]** CMS uses the lesser of methodology to determine payment for Cimzia when identified as a self‑administered product in the July 2020 OIG report.  
  _source: <https://www.cms.gov/medicare/payment/part-b-drugs/asp-reporting>_
- **[Regulatory & Pricing Benchmarks]** CMS included noncovered, self-administered versions of Cimzia in Part B payment calculations, contributing to an extra $366 million paid by Medicare from 2014 through 2016.  
  _source: <https://oig.hhs.gov/reports/all/2020/loophole-in-drug-payment-rule-continues-to-cost-medicare-and-beneficiaries-hundreds-of-millions-of-dollars/>_
- **[Financial Condition]** Closing the payment loophole for Cimzia would have saved Medicare and beneficiaries nearly half a billion dollars in 2017 and 2018.  
  _source: <https://oig.hhs.gov/reports/all/2020/loophole-in-drug-payment-rule-continues-to-cost-medicare-and-beneficiaries-hundreds-of-millions-of-dollars/>_
- **[Regulatory & Pricing Benchmarks]** The payment loophole may give physicians substantial incentives to administer Cimzia instead of other drugs for the same conditions.  
  _source: <https://oig.hhs.gov/reports/all/2020/loophole-in-drug-payment-rule-continues-to-cost-medicare-and-beneficiaries-hundreds-of-millions-of-dollars/>_
