## Rationale by Dimension

### Financial Condition (+2.0)

Mount Sinai’s Icahn School of Medicine has a licensing agreement with Janssen that provides payments for esketamine patents, indicating a source of revenue independent of payer volume. This suggests Mount Sinai can likely walk away from a sub‑optimal deal, favoring the hospital.

### Regulatory & Pricing Benchmarks (-3.0)

The FDA approval of SPRAVATO establishes a benchmark for pricing and reimbursement negotiations, giving Cigna a reference point to push rates down. The CIII classification also imposes specific scheduling and pricing regulations that can constrain pricing flexibility.

### Clinical Uniqueness (+4.0)

Esketamine is the first new mechanism of action in decades for major depressive disorder, delivered as a nasal spray with rapid symptom relief for treatment‑resistant depression. Its unique therapeutic profile makes it a must‑have provider in the market, favoring Mount Sinai.

- **[Regulatory & Pricing Benchmarks]** SPRAVATO (esketamine) was approved by the FDA on March 5, 2019 as a CIII nasal spray for treatment-resistant depression.

- **[Clinical Uniqueness]** Esketamine is the first new mechanism of action in decades to treat major depressive disorder, acting on the NMDA receptor.  
  _source: <https://inside.mountsinai.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/14/2019/03/Inside_March_18-–-31_2019_FOR-BLOG.pdf>_
- **[Clinical Uniqueness]** Esketamine is delivered as a nasal spray, providing a novel route of administration for depression treatment.  
  _source: <https://inside.mountsinai.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/14/2019/03/Inside_March_18-–-31_2019_FOR-BLOG.pdf>_
- **[Clinical Uniqueness]** Esketamine is indicated for treatment-resistant depression and has been shown to provide rapid symptom relief compared to traditional antidepressants.  
  _source: <https://inside.mountsinai.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/14/2019/03/Inside_March_18-–-31_2019_FOR-BLOG.pdf>_
- **[Clinical Uniqueness]** Esketamine targets the NMDA receptor, unlike other antidepressants that target serotonin or norepinephrine.
