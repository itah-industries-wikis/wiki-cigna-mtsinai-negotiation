## Rationale by Dimension

### Clinical Uniqueness (+3.0)

Ocrelizumab is a specialty monoclonal antibody that is the first proven treatment for primary progressive multiple sclerosis and is used at Mount Sinai’s dedicated MS center, indicating a tertiary/specialty service where Mount Sinai is a key provider.

## Supporting Facts

- **[Clinical Uniqueness]** Ocrelizumab significantly reduced progression of clinical disability in primary progressive multiple sclerosis at both 12 and 24 weeks in the ORATORIO trial.  
  _source: <https://inside.mountsinai.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/14/2018/03/Inside_January-9-22_2017.pdf>_
- **[Clinical Uniqueness]** Ocrelizumab resulted in 46 to 47 percent lower relapse rates than interferon beta-1a in relapsing multiple sclerosis in the OPERA I and OPERA II trials.  
  _source: <https://inside.mountsinai.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/14/2018/03/Inside_January-9-22_2017.pdf>_
- **[Clinical Uniqueness]** Ocrelizumab is a humanized monoclonal antibody that binds CD-20 proteins on B cells, causing their depletion.  
  _source: <https://inside.mountsinai.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/14/2018/03/Inside_January-9-22_2017.pdf>_
- **[Clinical Uniqueness]** Ocrelizumab is administered by intravenous infusion every six months.  
  _source: <https://inside.mountsinai.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/14/2018/03/Inside_January-9-22_2017.pdf>_
- **[Clinical Uniqueness]** Ocrelizumab is the first proven treatment for primary progressive multiple sclerosis.  
  _source: <https://inside.mountsinai.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/14/2018/03/Inside_January-9-22_2017.pdf>_
- **[Clinical Uniqueness]** Ocrelizumab is used to treat both primary progressive and relapsing forms of multiple sclerosis.  
  _source: <https://inside.mountsinai.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/14/2018/03/Inside_January-9-22_2017.pdf>_
- **[Clinical Uniqueness]** Ocrelizumab is manufactured by Genentech, a member of the Roche Group.  
  _source: <https://inside.mountsinai.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/14/2018/03/Inside_January-9-22_2017.pdf>_
- **[Clinical Uniqueness]** Ocrelizumab was an investigational drug at the time of the NEJM publication in December 2016.  
  _source: <https://inside.mountsinai.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/14/2018/03/Inside_January-9-22_2017.pdf>_
- **[Clinical Uniqueness]** Mount Sinai’s Corinne Goldsmith Dickinson Center for Multiple Sclerosis was a trial site in the first pivotal Phase 3 study to show efficacy in treating primary progressive MS with ocrelizumab.  
  _source: <https://inside.mountsinai.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/14/2018/03/Inside-March21-April-3.pdf>_
