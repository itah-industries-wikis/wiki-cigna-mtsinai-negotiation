## Rationale by Dimension

### Regulatory & Pricing Benchmarks (-4.0)

CMS’s lesser‑of methodology provides a clear benchmark for Orencia payment, and the identified payment loophole (extra $366 M 2014‑2016, nearly $500 M 2017‑2018) shows that Medicare is overpaying. Physicians rarely administer the self‑injected version, reducing the argument for higher rates. These facts give Cigna strong leverage to push rates down.

## Supporting Facts

- **[Regulatory & Pricing Benchmarks]** CMS uses the lesser of methodology to determine payment for Orencia when identified as a self‑administered product in the July 2020 OIG report.  
  _source: <https://www.cms.gov/medicare/payment/part-b-drugs/asp-reporting>_
- **[Regulatory & Pricing Benchmarks]** CMS included noncovered, self-administered versions of Orencia in Part B payment calculations, causing Medicare to pay an extra $366 million from 2014 through 2016.  
  _source: <https://oig.hhs.gov/reports/all/2020/loophole-in-drug-payment-rule-continues-to-cost-medicare-and-beneficiaries-hundreds-of-millions-of-dollars/>_
- **[Financial Condition]** Closing the payment loophole for Orencia would have saved Medicare and beneficiaries nearly half a billion dollars in 2017 and 2018.  
  _source: <https://oig.hhs.gov/reports/all/2020/loophole-in-drug-payment-rule-continues-to-cost-medicare-and-beneficiaries-hundreds-of-millions-of-dollars/>_
- **[Regulatory & Pricing Benchmarks]** Physicians almost never administered the self-injected version of Orencia to patients in their offices.  
  _source: <https://oig.hhs.gov/reports/all/2020/loophole-in-drug-payment-rule-continues-to-cost-medicare-and-beneficiaries-hundreds-of-millions-of-dollars/>_
- **[Regulatory & Pricing Benchmarks]** The payment loophole may give physicians substantial incentives to administer Orencia instead of other drugs for the same conditions.  
  _source: <https://oig.hhs.gov/reports/all/2020/loophole-in-drug-payment-rule-continues-to-cost-medicare-and-beneficiaries-hundreds-of-millions-of-dollars/>_
