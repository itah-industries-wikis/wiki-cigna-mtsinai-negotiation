## Rationale by Dimension

### Regulatory & Pricing Benchmarks (-5.0)

CMS’s lesser‑of methodology and the documented payment loophole for self‑administered Orencia provide a clear regulatory benchmark that Cigna can use to argue for lower rates. The evidence that closing the loophole would have saved Medicare and beneficiaries $497 million (22% of expenditures) from 2017‑2018 and that self‑administered versions cost $800‑$1,100 more per dose gives Cigna a strong basis to push rates down.

## Supporting Facts

- **[Regulatory & Pricing Benchmarks]** CMS uses the lesser of methodology to determine payment for Orencia when identified as a self‑administered product in the July 2020 OIG report.  
  _source: <https://www.cms.gov/medicare/payment/part-b-drugs/asp-reporting>_
- **[Regulatory & Pricing Benchmarks]** CMS included noncovered, self-administered versions of Orencia in Part B payment calculations, causing Medicare to pay an extra $366 million from 2014 through 2016.  
  _source: <https://oig.hhs.gov/reports/all/2020/loophole-in-drug-payment-rule-continues-to-cost-medicare-and-beneficiaries-hundreds-of-millions-of-dollars/>_
- **[Financial Condition]** Closing the payment loophole for Orencia would have saved Medicare and beneficiaries nearly half a billion dollars in 2017 and 2018.  
  _source: <https://oig.hhs.gov/reports/all/2020/loophole-in-drug-payment-rule-continues-to-cost-medicare-and-beneficiaries-hundreds-of-millions-of-dollars/>_
- **[Regulatory & Pricing Benchmarks]** Physicians almost never administered the self-injected version of Orencia to patients in their offices.  
  _source: <https://oig.hhs.gov/reports/all/2020/loophole-in-drug-payment-rule-continues-to-cost-medicare-and-beneficiaries-hundreds-of-millions-of-dollars/>_
- **[Regulatory & Pricing Benchmarks]** The payment loophole may give physicians substantial incentives to administer Orencia instead of other drugs for the same conditions.  
  _source: <https://oig.hhs.gov/reports/all/2020/loophole-in-drug-payment-rule-continues-to-cost-medicare-and-beneficiaries-hundreds-of-millions-of-dollars/>_
- **[Financial Condition]** CMS included noncovered self-administered versions of Orencia in Part B payment calculations, resulting in an extra $366 million paid by Medicare and beneficiaries from 2014 through 2016.  
  _source: <https://oig.hhs.gov/documents/evaluation/3210/OEI-BL-20-00100-Complete%20Report.pdf>_
- **[Financial Condition]** Twenty percent ($73.2 million) of the $366 million extra paid for Orencia from 2014‑2016 stemmed from additional coinsurance owed by Medicare beneficiaries.  
  _source: <https://oig.hhs.gov/documents/evaluation/3210/OEI-BL-20-00100-Complete%20Report.pdf>_
- **[Regulatory & Pricing Benchmarks]** Closing the payment loophole for Orencia would have reduced Medicare and beneficiary spending by $497 million (22% of expenditures) from 2017 through 2018.  
  _source: <https://oig.hhs.gov/documents/evaluation/3210/OEI-BL-20-00100-Complete%20Report.pdf>_
- **[Regulatory & Pricing Benchmarks]** When the self‑administered prefilled syringe version of Orencia was added in 2014, Medicare’s payment amount for the drug jumped by 35%.  
  _source: <https://oig.hhs.gov/documents/evaluation/3210/OEI-BL-20-00100-Complete%20Report.pdf>_
- **[Regulatory & Pricing Benchmarks]** If CMS had excluded self‑administered versions of Orencia, the quarterly Medicare payment amount would have been 23% to 27% lower from January 2017 through December 2018.  
  _source: <https://oig.hhs.gov/documents/evaluation/3210/OEI-BL-20-00100-Complete%20Report.pdf>_
- **[Financial Condition]** Medicare and beneficiaries would have saved $394 million over two years if payment amounts for Orencia had been set using only physician‑administered versions.  
  _source: <https://oig.hhs.gov/documents/evaluation/3210/OEI-BL-20-00100-Complete%20Report.pdf>_
- **[Financial Condition]** The payment loophole caused Orencia to cost approximately $800 to $1,100 more per dose compared to physician‑administered versions.  
  _source: <https://oig.hhs.gov/documents/evaluation/3210/OEI-BL-20-00100-Complete%20Report.pdf>_
- **[Financial Condition]** A typical Medicare beneficiary could face almost $3,000 in additional out‑of‑pocket spending per year due to the Orencia payment loophole.  
  _source: <https://oig.hhs.gov/documents/evaluation/3210/OEI-BL-20-00100-Complete%20Report.pdf>_
- **[unclassified]** Approximately 27,000 Medicare beneficiaries had at least one claim for Orencia paid under Part B in 2018.  
  _source: <https://oig.hhs.gov/documents/evaluation/3210/OEI-BL-20-00100-Complete%20Report.pdf>_
- **[Regulatory & Pricing Benchmarks]** CMS continues to include noncovered self‑administered versions of Orencia in Part B payment calculations as of December 2019.  
  _source: <https://oig.hhs.gov/documents/evaluation/3210/OEI-BL-20-00100-Complete%20Report.pdf>_
