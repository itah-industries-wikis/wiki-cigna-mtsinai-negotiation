### Clinical Uniqueness (+5.0)

Pralsetinib delivers a dramatic survival benefit (median OS 44 months vs 4–11 months) and high response rates (78% in treatment‑naïve, 63% post‑chemo, 73% with brain mets) in a rare RET‑fusion NSCLC population, making it a highly specialized, must‑have therapy that Mount Sinai can uniquely provide.

## Supporting Facts

- **[Clinical Uniqueness]** Pralsetinib extended median overall survival to 44 months for patients with advanced RET fusion‑positive non‑small cell lung cancer, compared to the expected 4–11 months before selective RET inhibitors were available.  
  _source: <https://www.massgeneralbrigham.org/en/about/newsroom/press-releases/targeted-therapy-improves-outcomes-for-lung-cancer-with-rare-mutations>_
- **[Clinical Uniqueness]** In the ARROW phase 1/2 study, the overall response rate to pralsetinib was 78% in patients who had not received prior treatment, 63% in patients who had previously undergone chemotherapy, and 73% in patients with brain metastases.  
  _source: <https://www.massgeneralbrigham.org/en/about/newsroom/press-releases/targeted-therapy-improves-outcomes-for-lung-cancer-with-rare-mutations>_
- **[Clinical Uniqueness]** The median duration of response to pralsetinib was 47.9 months for patients with CCDC6‑RET fusion and 13.1 months for those with KIF5B‑RET fusion.  
  _source: <https://www.massgeneralbrigham.org/en/about/newsroom/press-releases/targeted-therapy-improves-outcomes-for-lung-cancer-with-rare-mutations>_
- **[Clinical Uniqueness]** Treatment with pralsetinib led to discontinuation in 10% of patients due to adverse effects and required dose reduction in 51% of patients.  
  _source: <https://www.massgeneralbrigham.org/en/about/newsroom/press-releases/targeted-therapy-improves-outcomes-for-lung-cancer-with-rare-mutations>_
- **[Clinical Uniqueness]** Pralsetinib is an FDA‑approved selective RET inhibitor with a manageable safety profile and no hypersensitivity reactions in patients previously exposed to immunotherapies.  
  _source: <https://www.massgeneralbrigham.org/en/about/newsroom/press-releases/targeted-therapy-improves-outcomes-for-lung-cancer-with-rare-mutations>_
- **[Clinical Uniqueness]** The ARROW study enrolled 281 patients with advanced or metastatic RET fusion‑positive NSCLC and included 42 months of follow‑up data.  
  _source: <https://www.massgeneralbrigham.org/en/about/newsroom/press-releases/targeted-therapy-improves-outcomes-for-lung-cancer-with-rare-mutations>_
