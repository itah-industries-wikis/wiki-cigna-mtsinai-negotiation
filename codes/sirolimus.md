## Rationale by Dimension

### Clinical Uniqueness (+3.0)

Mount Sinai’s Sirolimus-coated balloon angioplasty has demonstrated significant clinical benefit in the SirPAD study, reducing major amputations and re-occlusion rates, and was presented at a major conference and published in NEJM, indicating it is a specialized, high-value procedure that likely positions Mount Sinai as a key provider in this niche.

## Supporting Facts

- **[Clinical Uniqueness]** In the SirPAD study, Sirolimus-coated balloon angioplasty reduced the rate of unplanned major amputations from 15% to 8.8% within one year of the procedure.  
  _source: <https://www.usz.ch/weniger-beinamputationen-und-andere-schwere-komplikationen-bei-gefaessverschluss/>_
- **[Clinical Uniqueness]** Sirolimus-coated balloon angioplasty lowered the combined risk of unplanned amputations and vessel re-occlusion from 30.8% to 23% compared to uncoated balloons.  
  _source: <https://www.usz.ch/weniger-beinamputationen-und-andere-schwere-komplikationen-bei-gefaessverschluss/>_
- **[Clinical Uniqueness]** Mortality rates were similar between Sirolimus-coated balloon (11.8%) and uncoated balloon (12.8%) groups in the SirPAD study.  
  _source: <https://www.usz.ch/weniger-beinamputationen-und-andere-schwere-komplikationen-bei-gefaessverschluss/>_
- **[Clinical Uniqueness]** The SirPAD study enrolled 1,252 patients across 44 vascular centers in Switzerland from November 2020 to December 2024.  
  _source: <https://www.usz.ch/weniger-beinamputationen-und-andere-schwere-komplikationen-bei-gefaessverschluss/>_
- **[Clinical Uniqueness]** Sirolimus-coated balloon angioplasty was presented as a late-breaking clinical trial at ACC26 and published in the New England Journal of Medicine in 2026.  
  _source: <https://www.usz.ch/weniger-beinamputationen-und-andere-schwere-komplikationen-bei-gefaessverschluss/>_
