## Rationale by Dimension

### Clinical Uniqueness (-3.0)

tPA is a generic clot‑busting drug used for both stroke and CRAO, and the infusion into the ophthalmic artery is a specialized use but not exclusive to Mount Sinai. The drug is widely available across many providers, giving Cigna leverage to negotiate rates rather than being forced to include Mount Sinai as a must‑have provider.

## Supporting Facts

- **[Clinical Uniqueness]** tPA must be started within three to four-and-a-half hours of the onset of stroke symptoms.  
  _source: <https://inside.mountsinai.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/14/2018/03/InsideMountSinaiMay2015.pdf>_
- **[unclassified]** tPA (tissue plasminogen activator) is the clot‑busting drug used for CRAO treatment.  
  _source: <https://www.nyee.edu/files/MSHealth/Assets/NYEE/NYEE-Opth-Mailer-2023.pdf>_
- **[Clinical Uniqueness]** tPA infusion into the ophthalmic artery within 12 hours of central retinal artery occlusion is safe and leads to significant visual improvement (published 2021).  
  _source: <https://www.nyee.edu/files/MSHealth/Assets/NYEE/NYEE-EyeStrokePrgrm-E-Flyer.pdf>_
